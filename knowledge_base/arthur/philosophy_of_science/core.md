# Philosophy of Science: Evidence, Models, and Explanation

## 1. Theory name
Falsificationism, paradigms, research programmes, realism debates, and causal inference.
## 2. Proponents
Karl Popper; Thomas Kuhn; Imre Lakatos; Bas van Fraassen; contemporary philosophers of science.
## 3. Historical background
Twentieth-century philosophy examined demarcation, theory change, and the relation between scientific models and reality.
## 4. Core concepts
Falsifiability; corroboration; paradigm; normal science; anomaly; progressive/degenerating research programme; realism; underdetermination; causal identification.
## 5. Basic claims
Popper emphasized risky testable predictions; Kuhn described paradigm-guided science and revolutions; Lakatos evaluated programmes over time. These accounts differ and do not mean scientists simply discard every contradicted theory.
## 6. Research findings
Science uses diverse methods, replication, measurement, modeling, and peer criticism. Causal claims require designs and assumptions that address confounding; correlation alone is insufficient.
## 7. Classic research
Popper (1959), *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*; Kuhn (1962), *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*; Lakatos (1978), *The Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes*. These are philosophical works, not experiments.
## 8. Scope
Useful for grading claims by source quality, testability, replication, causal design, and scope of inference.
## 9. Limitations
No single demarcation rule captures all science; historical case interpretations are debated. A theory's testability does not by itself establish truth.
## 10. Applied example
For a claim that an intervention improves mood, ask whether studies compare suitable controls, measure outcomes consistently, replicate, and distinguish correlation from causal effect.
## 11. Related theories
Epistemology; statistics; causal inference; scientific realism; sociology of knowledge.
## 12. References and evidence status
Popper (1959), *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* [philosophy]; Kuhn (1962), *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* [history/philosophy]; Lakatos (1978), *The Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes* [philosophy]. Treat as interpretive frameworks, not empirical laws.
