# Epistemology: Knowledge, Evidence, and Justification

## 1. Theory name
Analytic epistemology, evidentialism, foundationalism, coherentism, reliabilism, and skepticism.
## 2. Proponents
Plato; René Descartes; David Hume; Edmund Gettier; Alvin Goldman; contemporary epistemologists.
## 3. Historical background
Questions about knowledge and justification run from ancient Greek philosophy through modern rationalism/empiricism to contemporary analytic debates.
## 4. Core concepts
Belief, truth, justification, evidence, defeaters, reliability, testimony, skepticism, induction, observation versus interpretation.
## 5. Basic claims
Knowing is commonly treated as more than believing truly; Gettier cases challenged the simple justified-true-belief account. Evidence supports degrees of confidence and can be defeated by new information.
## 6. Research findings
Epistemology is primarily conceptual and normative philosophy, not an experimentally settled psychological science. Cognitive science can study how people form beliefs but cannot alone define knowledge.
## 7. Classic research
Gettier (1963), “Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?” *Analysis*, 23, 121–123, presented counterexamples. Hume (1748), *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, examines induction.
## 8. Scope
Useful for asking what would justify a claim, what evidence is missing, and how observation differs from inference.
## 9. Limitations
No consensus definition resolves all cases. Demanding certainty can be unreasonable; accepting claims without calibrated evidence is also risky.
## 10. Applied example
“They did not answer, therefore they dislike me” moves from an observation to one causal interpretation; list alternatives and evidence that could discriminate them.
## 11. Related theories
Formal logic; Bayesian epistemology; philosophy of science; social epistemology; cognitive bias research.
## 12. References and evidence status
Plato, *Theaetetus* [historical dialogue]; Hume (1748), *Enquiry* [philosophical argument]; Gettier (1963) [canonical counterexamples]; Goldman (1979), “What Is Justified Belief?” [reliabilist theory]. Philosophical positions remain contested.
