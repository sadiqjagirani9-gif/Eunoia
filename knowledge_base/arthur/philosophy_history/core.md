# History of Philosophy: Traditions and Context

## 1. Theory name
Historical traditions: ancient Greek, medieval, early modern, German classical, analytic, phenomenological, existential, and early Chinese philosophy.
## 2. Proponents
Examples include Plato, Aristotle, Augustine, Aquinas, Descartes, Hume, Kant, Hegel, Frege, Wittgenstein, Husserl, Sartre, Confucius, Laozi, and Zhuangzi.
## 3. Historical background
Philosophical questions developed in distinct linguistic, religious, political, and institutional contexts; traditions are internally diverse and not a single linear progression.
## 4. Core concepts
Being, knowledge, virtue, reason, language, freedom, experience, governance, ritual, the Way, and human flourishing.
## 5. Basic claims
Historical interpretation asks what a thinker argued in context before applying a concept today. Similar words across traditions do not guarantee identical concepts.
## 6. Research findings
History of philosophy is interpretive scholarship grounded in primary texts and contextual research; it is not experimental science. Translations and scholarly readings can disagree.
## 7. Classic research
Primary sources are the classic works: Plato's *Republic*, Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics*, Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason*, Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations*, Confucius' *Analects*, and the *Daodejing*.
## 8. Scope
Use historical concepts to broaden framing, not to imply an ancient thinker directly answers a modern empirical question.
## 9. Limitations
Selective quotation and presentism distort arguments; attributions may be contested, especially where texts are composite or transmitted over time.
## 10. Applied example
Use Stoic distinctions about control as a reflective prompt, while acknowledging that structural constraints and interdependence can make “control” more complex than individual choice.
## 11. Related theories
Ethics; epistemology; political philosophy; intellectual history; comparative philosophy.
## 12. References and evidence status
Primary works listed above; Kenny (2010), *A New History of Western Philosophy* [secondary survey]; Van Norden (2011), *Introduction to Classical Chinese Philosophy* [secondary survey]. Interpretations should identify tradition and historical context.
