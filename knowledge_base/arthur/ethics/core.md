# Ethics: Consequences, Duties, Virtues, and Rights

## 1. Theory name
Consequentialism/utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, contractualism, and rights theories.
## 2. Proponents
Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill; Immanuel Kant; Aristotle; T. M. Scanlon; contemporary moral philosophers.
## 3. Historical background
Ethical theory developed from ancient virtue traditions through Enlightenment accounts of utility, duty, autonomy, and modern contractualist debate.
## 4. Core concepts
Consequences; welfare; duty; universalization; virtue; practical wisdom; consent; rights; fairness; responsibility.
## 5. Basic claims
Utilitarian views assess outcomes; Kantian approaches emphasize duties and persons; virtue ethics asks what character and practical wisdom require; contractualism examines principles no one could reasonably reject.
## 6. Research findings
Ethics is normative philosophy. Empirical psychology can inform how people judge and act but cannot by itself settle what is morally right.
## 7. Classic research
Mill (1861), *Utilitarianism*; Kant (1785), *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*; Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*; Scanlon (1998), *What We Owe to Each Other*.
## 8. Scope
Useful for exposing value trade-offs in dilemmas involving harm, promise, fairness, autonomy, and care.
## 9. Limitations
Theories disagree on foundations and can yield conflicting prescriptions; outcomes and rights may be difficult to measure or balance. Do not disguise a value premise as a factual conclusion.
## 10. Applied example
When deciding whether to disclose a painful truth, compare likely harms, duties of honesty, respect for agency, and the character of a trustworthy response.
## 11. Related theories
Political philosophy; moral psychology; law; care ethics; decision theory.
## 12. References and evidence status
Mill (1861), *Utilitarianism* [normative theory]; Kant (1785), *Groundwork* [normative theory]; Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* [virtue tradition]; Scanlon (1998) [contractualism]. These are philosophical arguments, not experimentally verified facts.
