# Attachment Theory Across Development and Relationships

## 1. Theory name
Attachment theory and adult attachment research.
## 2. Proponents
John Bowlby; Mary Ainsworth; later adult attachment researchers include Cindy Hazan, Phillip Shaver, Kim Bartholomew, and Mario Mikulincer.
## 3. Historical background
Bowlby integrated ethology, developmental observation, and psychoanalytic questions in the mid-twentieth century. Ainsworth developed observational research on infant-caregiver relationships.
## 4. Core concepts
Proximity seeking; secure base; safe haven; internal working models; caregiver sensitivity; attachment-related anxiety and avoidance in some adult models.
## 5. Basic claims
Attachment behavior is an adaptive system for seeking protection and connection. Experiences can shape expectations, but development remains contextual and relationships can differ across partners and time.
## 6. Research findings
Caregiver sensitivity is associated with attachment security, but associations are probabilistic and shaped by many factors. Adult attachment measures describe dimensions or patterns, not diagnoses or immutable identities.
## 7. Classic research
Ainsworth et al. (1978), *Patterns of Attachment*, documented the Strange Situation procedure. Hazan & Shaver (1987), “Romantic Love Conceptualized as an Attachment Process,” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 52, 511–524, applied attachment ideas to adult romantic love.
## 8. Scope
May help formulate questions about seeking closeness, responding to separation, trust, and emotion regulation in relationships.
## 9. Limitations
Measures and classifications have cultural and methodological limits; infant categories cannot be directly assigned to adults. Never infer a user's or another person's type from one behavior.
## 10. Applied example
If a person asks for reassurance repeatedly, consider uncertainty, recent events, communication norms, and attachment-related needs; do not label them anxious from this behavior alone.
## 11. Related theories
Emotion regulation; caregiving; interpersonal models; object relations; self-determination and relationship science.
## 12. References and evidence status
Bowlby (1969/1982), *Attachment and Loss, Vol. 1* [foundational theory]; Ainsworth et al. (1978), *Patterns of Attachment* [observational research]; Hazan & Shaver (1987) [early adult attachment study]; Fraley & Shaver (2000), “Adult Romantic Attachment,” *Review of General Psychology*, 4, 132–154 [review].
