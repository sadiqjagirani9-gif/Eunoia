# Close Relationships: Intimacy, Conflict, and Change

## 1. Theory name
Close-relationship science, interdependence, investment model, self-disclosure, and conflict research.
## 2. Proponents
Harold Kelley and John Thibaut; Caryl Rusbult; Elaine Hatfield; Arthur Aron; John Gottman and many other researchers.
## 3. Historical background
Relationship research grew from social psychology and interpersonal theory, studying attraction, commitment, communication, conflict, and relationship maintenance.
## 4. Core concepts
Interdependence; rewards and costs; comparison level; investment; commitment; responsiveness; self-disclosure; boundaries; power and repair.
## 5. Basic claims
Relationship quality depends on interacting expectations, behavior, resources, alternatives, communication, and context. One person's affection does not establish the other's feelings; reciprocity requires evidence.
## 6. Research findings
Commitment and satisfaction correlate with several relationship processes, but associations do not determine an individual future. Communication patterns can predict outcomes in some samples, not guarantee them.
## 7. Classic research
Rusbult (1980), “Commitment and Satisfaction in Romantic Associations,” *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology*, 16, 172–186, tested the investment model. Aron et al. (1997), “The Experimental Generation of Interpersonal Closeness,” *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 23, 363–377, studied structured self-disclosure.
## 8. Scope
Helps examine needs, boundaries, mutuality, conflict cycles, commitment, and possible relationship choices.
## 9. Limitations
Relationship studies often use self-report and selected populations. Popular claims about universal “stages” or predictive formulas overstate evidence. No model can infer a partner's private thoughts from a single interaction.
## 10. Applied example
For repeated cancellations, distinguish stated reasons, observed pattern, impact, and desired boundary; discuss what evidence would change the user's view of reciprocity.
## 11. Related theories
Attachment; social exchange; interdependence; communication; family systems; conflict and negotiation research.
## 12. References and evidence status
Kelley & Thibaut (1978), *Interpersonal Relations: A Theory of Interdependence* [theory]; Rusbult (1980) [empirical model]; Aron et al. (1997) [experiment]; Reis & Shaver (1988), “Intimacy as an Interpersonal Process” [framework]. Relationship outcomes remain context-dependent.
