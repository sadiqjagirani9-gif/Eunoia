# Cognitive-Behavioral Theory and Evidence-Based Practice

## 1. Theory name
Cognitive therapy, rational-emotive approaches, and cognitive-behavioral models.
## 2. Proponents
Aaron T. Beck; Albert Ellis; later CBT researchers and clinicians.
## 3. Historical background
CBT developed through mid-twentieth-century clinical research, integrating learning theory and cognitive models. It now includes varied protocols rather than a single method.
## 4. Core concepts
Automatic thoughts; assumptions; core beliefs; appraisal; behavioral reinforcement; avoidance; exposure; skills practice; cognitive restructuring.
## 5. Basic claims
Interpretations, emotions, bodily responses, and actions influence one another. Testing a thought or changing a behavior can provide new information; the goal is not to deny real harm or force positivity.
## 6. Research findings
CBT has substantial outcome evidence for several defined clinical conditions, with efficacy varying by condition, protocol, access, and person. Evidence for treatment does not mean every everyday problem is a disorder.
## 7. Classic research
Beck et al. (1979), *Cognitive Therapy of Depression*, described the model and practice. Ellis (1962), *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy*, developed rational-emotive therapy. These are foundational texts; contemporary treatment claims require current reviews.
## 8. Scope
Useful for separating event from interpretation, identifying recurring thought-action loops, and designing small behavioral experiments.
## 9. Limitations
Thought records can oversimplify structural realities or emotion; changing beliefs is not always the main need. Avoid labeling every inference a “distortion” or treating reflection as therapy.
## 10. Applied example
“They did not reply, so I am unimportant” can be examined for evidence, alternatives, and impact; a small direct check may generate information without pretending rejection is impossible.
## 11. Related theories
Learning theory; appraisal; metacognition; behavioral activation; acceptance-based approaches; emotion regulation.
## 12. References and evidence status
Beck et al. (1979), *Cognitive Therapy of Depression* [foundational clinical text]; Ellis (1962), *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy* [historical framework]; Hofmann et al. (2012), “The Efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy,” *Cognitive Therapy and Research*, 36, 427–440 [review; condition-specific].
