# General Psychology: Emotion, Cognition, and Motivation

## 1. Theory name
Core topics: appraisal, motivation, attention, memory, learning, attribution, and individual differences.
## 2. Proponents
William James, Wilhelm Wundt, Kurt Lewin, Richard Lazarus, and contemporary cognitive and social psychologists; no single unified theory owns this field.
## 3. Historical background
Experimental psychology developed in the late nineteenth century, followed by behaviorist, cognitive, and social-cognitive approaches. Current psychology uses multiple methods and levels of explanation.
## 4. Core concepts
Appraisal; goals and needs; attention; working and long-term memory; reinforcement; attribution; person-situation interaction; individual differences.
## 5. Basic claims
Emotion and behavior arise through interactions among bodily states, interpretation, goals, learning history, social context, and situation. A person's explanation of an event is not identical to the event itself.
## 6. Research findings
Evidence supports interactions between appraisal, physiology, attention, memory, and action tendencies. Effects vary by task and context; no single mechanism fully explains every emotion or choice.
## 7. Classic research
James (1890), *The Principles of Psychology*, is a historical synthesis. Lazarus (1991), *Emotion and Adaptation*, develops appraisal theory. These are foundational works, not a single decisive experiment.
## 8. Scope
Useful for mapping situation, interpretation, goal, emotion, action, and consequences in everyday reflection.
## 9. Limitations
Laboratory tasks simplify life; self-report is fallible; cultural and individual differences matter. Do not infer unconscious causes or stable traits from one account.
## 10. Applied example
After a delayed reply, separate the observable delay from interpretations such as rejection; identify the threatened goal (connection or certainty), emotion, and possible next action.
## 11. Related theories
Emotion appraisal; attribution theory; reinforcement learning; cognitive-behavioral models; social-cognitive person-situation models.
## 12. References and evidence status
James (1890), *The Principles of Psychology* [historical synthesis]; Lazarus (1991), *Emotion and Adaptation* [theoretical synthesis]; Lewin (1936), *Principles of Topological Psychology* [historical theory]. Concepts are broad frameworks; specific claims need task-specific evidence.
