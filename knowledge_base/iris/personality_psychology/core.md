# Personality Psychology: Traits, Development, and Context

## 1. Theory name
Trait theories, Big Five model, person-situation interaction, and personality development.
## 2. Proponents
Gordon Allport; Raymond Cattell; Paul Costa and Robert McCrae; Walter Mischel; contemporary personality researchers.
## 3. Historical background
Personality psychology moved from typologies to measured traits, longitudinal development, and interactionist accounts of person and situation.
## 4. Core concepts
Extraversion, agreeableness, conscientiousness, neuroticism/emotional stability, openness; traits; states; stability; change; person-situation interaction.
## 5. Basic claims
Traits summarize regularities across behavior, but situation and development matter. A trait score is not a full explanation of a person or a moral judgment.
## 6. Research findings
Trait measures show meaningful stability and change over time, with age, context, and measurement affecting estimates. Self-report is only one perspective.
## 7. Classic research
Costa & McCrae (1992), *Revised NEO Personality Inventory*; Roberts, Walton & Viechtbauer (2006), “Patterns of Mean-Level Change in Personality Traits Across the Life Course,” *Psychological Bulletin*, 132, 1–25.
## 8. Scope
Useful for discussing recurring tendencies over multiple situations while preserving individual variability.
## 9. Limitations
Traits are descriptive and do not establish causes. Cultural validity, self-knowledge, and context constrain measures; never infer a stable profile from a single event.
## 10. Applied example
If someone reports avoiding conflict, ask whether this repeats across relationships and settings, and look for contexts where they communicate directly.
## 11. Related theories
Temperament; identity; social-cognitive theory; narrative identity; developmental psychology.
## 12. References and evidence status
Allport (1937), *Personality: A Psychological Interpretation* [historical]; Costa & McCrae (1992) [measurement]; Roberts et al. (2006) [meta-analysis]. Trait models are supported descriptive frameworks, not diagnoses.
