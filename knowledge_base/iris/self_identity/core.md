# Self and Identity: Narrative, Evaluation, and Agency

## 1. Theory name
Self-concept, identity, self-efficacy, self-esteem, narrative identity, and possible selves.
## 2. Proponents
William James; Erik Erikson; Hazel Markus; Albert Bandura; Dan McAdams; contemporary self researchers.
## 3. Historical background
Self theories span philosophy and psychology; twentieth-century research operationalized self-concept, efficacy, identity, and autobiographical narrative.
## 4. Core concepts
Actual and ideal self; self-efficacy; self-esteem; social identity; possible selves; self-consistency; autobiographical narrative; agency.
## 5. Basic claims
Self-understanding is multifaceted and context-sensitive. Narratives organize experience but can change; discrepancy between stated values and behavior may reflect constraints or competing goals.
## 6. Research findings
Self-efficacy relates to effort and persistence in many domains; self-esteem and narrative measures correlate with outcomes but do not reveal a single authentic self.
## 7. Classic research
James (1890), *The Principles of Psychology*; Bandura (1997), *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control*; McAdams (1993), *The Stories We Live By*.
## 8. Scope
Useful for exploring self-description, desired identity, feared identity, and how a past event shapes present self-evaluation.
## 9. Limitations
Self-report is shaped by context and audience; “true self” claims can become unfalsifiable. The AI's reading is not more authoritative than the user's own account.
## 10. Applied example
After a failed presentation, distinguish “I performed poorly this time” from “I am incapable,” and explore what evidence, context, and future action matter.
## 11. Related theories
Personality; social identity; self-determination; cognitive-behavioral models; existentialism.
## 12. References and evidence status
James (1890) [historical]; Bandura (1997) [self-efficacy synthesis]; Markus & Nurius (1986), “Possible Selves,” *American Psychologist*, 41, 954–969 [theory]; McAdams (1993) [narrative framework].
