# Self-Determination Theory: Motivation and Psychological Needs

## 1. Theory name
Self-determination theory (SDT), including basic psychological needs and organismic integration.
## 2. Proponents
Edward Deci and Richard Ryan; contemporary SDT researchers.
## 3. Historical background
SDT grew from intrinsic-motivation experiments in the 1970s and developed into a broad theory of motivation and wellbeing.
## 4. Core concepts
Autonomy; competence; relatedness; intrinsic motivation; internalization; controlled and autonomous regulation.
## 5. Basic claims
Motivation quality is influenced by support for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Autonomy means volition, not isolation or doing everything alone.
## 6. Research findings
Need satisfaction is associated with motivation and wellbeing across many studies; effects and measures vary by culture, domain, and design. Correlation does not establish every causal claim.
## 7. Classic research
Deci (1971), “Effects of Externally Mediated Rewards on Intrinsic Motivation,” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 18, 105–115; Ryan & Deci (2000), “Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation,” *American Psychologist*, 55, 68–78.
## 8. Scope
Useful for exploring whether a goal feels chosen, manageable, and connected to relationships or contribution.
## 9. Limitations
Need measures and cross-cultural interpretations are debated; the framework does not explain every motive or remove material constraints.
## 10. Applied example
If a user cannot sustain a goal, ask whether it is personally meaningful, whether they have resources to feel capable, and whether social support is available.
## 11. Related theories
Goal theory; behavior change; humanistic psychology; motivation; education.
## 12. References and evidence status
Deci (1971) [classic experiment]; Ryan & Deci (2000) [theory review]; Deci & Ryan (2000), “The ‘What’ and ‘Why’ of Goal Pursuits,” *Psychological Inquiry*, 11, 227–268 [framework]. Empirical support is substantial but domain- and method-sensitive.
