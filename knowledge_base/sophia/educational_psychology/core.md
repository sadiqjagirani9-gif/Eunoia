# Educational Psychology: Learning, Feedback, and Self-Efficacy

## 1. Theory name
Learning sciences, self-efficacy, motivation, cognitive load, retrieval practice, and feedback.
## 2. Proponents
Albert Bandura; John Sweller; Carol Dweck; Henry Roediger; contemporary educational researchers.
## 3. Historical background
Educational psychology combines experimental learning research, developmental psychology, and classroom studies.
## 4. Core concepts
Prior knowledge; working-memory load; retrieval; spacing; feedback; self-efficacy; mastery goals; motivation; transfer.
## 5. Basic claims
Learning improves when practice engages retrieval, feedback is informative, and task difficulty fits current knowledge. Beliefs matter but do not override access and instruction quality.
## 6. Research findings
Spacing and retrieval practice have broad support for retention; transfer depends on content and context. Growth-mindset interventions have heterogeneous and often modest effects.
## 7. Classic research
Roediger & Karpicke (2006), “Test-Enhanced Learning,” *Psychological Science*, 17, 249–255; Sweller (1988), “Cognitive Load During Problem Solving,” *Cognitive Science*, 12, 257–285.
## 8. Scope
Useful for study planning, skill acquisition, and diagnosing whether difficulty reflects method, prerequisite knowledge, or overload.
## 9. Limitations
Lab retention is not equivalent to broad expertise; classroom context and learner differences matter. Avoid blaming a learner for structural barriers.
## 10. Applied example
Replace repeated rereading with short retrieval sessions spaced across days, then use errors to select the next practice topic.
## 11. Related theories
Memory; motivation; development; behavior change; instructional design.
## 12. References and evidence status
Roediger & Karpicke (2006) [experiments]; Sweller (1988) [cognitive-load theory]; Dunlosky et al. (2013), “Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques,” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14, 4–58 [review].
