# Decision Theory: Trade-offs Under Uncertainty

## 1. Theory name
Normative decision theory, expected utility, multi-criteria choice, and real-options reasoning.
## 2. Proponents
Leonard Savage; John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern; Herbert Simon; contemporary decision analysts.
## 3. Historical background
Formal decision theory developed from probability and economics; bounded rationality later emphasized limited time, information, and computation.
## 4. Core concepts
Options; outcomes; utility; probabilities; opportunity cost; risk; sensitivity; reversibility; value of information; worst-case analysis.
## 5. Basic claims
A decision is evaluated relative to goals, consequences, uncertainty, and constraints. Expected utility is a normative tool under assumptions, not a prediction of human behavior.
## 6. Research findings
Structured decision aids can improve consistency and reveal trade-offs; uncertain probabilities and unstable preferences limit precision.
## 7. Classic research
Savage (1954), *The Foundations of Statistics*; von Neumann & Morgenstern (1944), *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*; Simon (1955), “A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice,” *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 69, 99–118.
## 8. Scope
Useful for comparing career, relationship, education, and resource decisions with no perfect option.
## 9. Limitations
Quantification may create false precision; hard-to-measure values and distributional effects matter. Never invent numeric probabilities when evidence is absent.
## 10. Applied example
For a job change, compare pay, learning, health, location, and reversibility; test sensitive assumptions (manager, savings runway) before treating a score as decisive.
## 11. Related theories
Behavioral economics; risk analysis; multi-criteria analysis; game theory; planning.
## 12. References and evidence status
Savage (1954) [normative framework]; Simon (1955) [bounded rationality]; Keeney & Raiffa (1976), *Decisions with Multiple Objectives* [method]. Models structure choices; they do not guarantee outcomes.
