# Behavioral Economics: Judgment and Choice

## 1. Theory name
Prospect theory, heuristics and biases, loss aversion, framing, and choice architecture.
## 2. Proponents
Daniel Kahneman; Amos Tversky; Richard Thaler; Cass Sunstein.
## 3. Historical background
Behavioral economics challenged models of perfectly consistent rational choice using psychological experiments and field studies.
## 4. Core concepts
Reference dependence; loss aversion; probability weighting; anchoring; endowment effect; sunk cost; status quo bias; mental accounting; present bias.
## 5. Basic claims
Choices can depend on framing and reference points; people often treat gains and losses asymmetrically. These are descriptive tendencies, not proof a particular decision is irrational.
## 6. Research findings
Many framing and judgment effects have experimental support, with variation across tasks, populations, and replications. Nudges can help or manipulate depending on design and goals.
## 7. Classic research
Kahneman & Tversky (1979), “Prospect Theory,” *Econometrica*, 47, 263–291; Thaler (1980), “Toward a Positive Theory of Consumer Choice,” *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 1, 39–60.
## 8. Scope
Useful for checking whether sunk investment, defaults, framing, or immediate rewards are distorting a consequential choice.
## 9. Limitations
Effect sizes and generalizability vary; “bias” does not mean every deviation from a formal model is harmful. Preferences may be legitimate and context-sensitive.
## 10. Applied example
Before staying in a costly program because of money already spent, compare only future costs and benefits; acknowledge grief about sunk investment without letting it dictate the next step.
## 11. Related theories
Decision theory; judgment under uncertainty; nudging; rational choice; self-control.
## 12. References and evidence status
Kahneman & Tversky (1979) [foundational experiment-based theory]; Thaler (1980) [theory]; Tversky & Kahneman (1974), “Judgment under Uncertainty,” *Science*, 185, 1124–1131 [classic review]. Findings are robust in some domains and debated in others.
