# Career Development: Choices, Skills, and Transitions

## 1. Theory name
Career construction, human capital, person-environment fit, career adaptability, and vocational decision-making.
## 2. Proponents
Donald Super; John Holland; Mark Savickas; Gary Becker; contemporary career researchers.
## 3. Historical background
Career theory moved from trait matching toward lifespan development, narrative, adaptability, labor-market context, and lifelong transitions.
## 4. Core concepts
Interests; skills; values; human capital; fit; adaptability; labor demand; networks; opportunity cost; transition risk.
## 5. Basic claims
Career decisions involve changing self-understanding and external opportunity structures. Skills can create options, but credentials and effort do not guarantee labor-market outcomes.
## 6. Research findings
Fit and adaptability relate to satisfaction and transitions in studied populations; labor markets, discrimination, geography, and economic cycles affect outcomes.
## 7. Classic research
Super (1980), “A Life-Span, Life-Space Approach to Career Development,” *Journal of Vocational Behavior*, 16, 282–298; Holland (1997), *Making Vocational Choices*; Savickas (2005), “The Theory and Practice of Career Construction.”
## 8. Scope
Useful for comparing work options, identifying skill gaps, and planning low-risk experiments before a major transition.
## 9. Limitations
Career models reflect cultural and economic assumptions; historical job data may become outdated. Local labor-market facts require current verification.
## 10. Applied example
Before changing fields, interview practitioners, complete a small project, estimate savings and training costs, and define what evidence would support a transition.
## 11. Related theories
Decision theory; sociology of work; education; organizational behavior; behavioral economics.
## 12. References and evidence status
Super (1980) [lifespan framework]; Holland (1997) [vocational theory]; Savickas (2005) [career construction]; Becker (1964), *Human Capital* [economic theory]. Outcomes remain contingent on context.
