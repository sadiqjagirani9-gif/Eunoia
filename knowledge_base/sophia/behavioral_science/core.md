# Behavioral Science: Habits, Motivation, and Context

## 1. Theory name
Behavioral science: learning, motivation, self-regulation, habit, and behavior change.
## 2. Proponents
B. F. Skinner; Albert Bandura; Wendy Wood; Peter Gollwitzer; contemporary behavioral scientists.
## 3. Historical background
Behaviorism emphasized observable learning; cognitive and social-cognitive research later incorporated goals, expectations, and self-regulation.
## 4. Core concepts
Reinforcement; cues and routines; implementation intentions; self-efficacy; friction; feedback; intrinsic/extrinsic motivation; delay.
## 5. Basic claims
Behavior reflects person, environment, learning, and immediate incentives. Knowledge of a goal alone often does not overcome friction, competing rewards, stress, or limited resources.
## 6. Research findings
Specific cues, feedback, and stable contexts can support habits, but effects vary and behavior change is rarely guaranteed by one technique.
## 7. Classic research
Bandura (1977), “Self-Efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral Change,” *Psychological Review*, 84, 191–215; Gollwitzer (1999), “Implementation Intentions,” *American Psychologist*, 54, 493–503.
## 8. Scope
Useful for turning intentions into small actions and diagnosing environmental friction rather than moral weakness.
## 9. Limitations
Habit models can understate poverty, disability, care burdens, institutional barriers, and changing goals. Laboratory tasks may not generalize.
## 10. Applied example
For a postponed task, specify a cue (“after breakfast”), reduce startup steps, and define a ten-minute first action; review whether the obstacle was time, energy, skill, or meaning.
## 11. Related theories
Behavioral economics; self-determination; goal setting; learning theory; implementation science.
## 12. References and evidence status
Bandura (1977) [theoretical review]; Gollwitzer (1999) [research review]; Wood & Neal (2007), “A New Look at Habits and the Habit-Goal Interface,” *Psychological Review*, 114, 843–863 [review]. Effects are context-sensitive.
