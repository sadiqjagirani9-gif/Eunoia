# Sociology: Institutions, Networks, and Social Structure

## 1. Theory name
Core sociological perspectives: social structure, networks, capital, norms, stratification, and institutions.
## 2. Proponents
Émile Durkheim; Max Weber; Pierre Bourdieu; Robert Putnam; contemporary sociologists.
## 3. Historical background
Sociology emerged in the nineteenth century amid industrialization, urbanization, and political change; later traditions study institutions, inequality, and interaction.
## 4. Core concepts
Socialization; status; class; social capital; norms; networks; institutions; mobility; role; structure and agency.
## 5. Basic claims
Individual choices occur within patterned opportunities and constraints. Structures shape behavior without mechanically determining each person's life.
## 6. Research findings
Longitudinal and comparative research documents associations between resources, networks, institutions, and outcomes; causal interpretation depends on study design and context.
## 7. Classic research
Durkheim (1897), *Suicide*, used comparative statistics; Bourdieu (1986), “The Forms of Capital,” developed capital distinctions; Granovetter (1973), “The Strength of Weak Ties,” *AJS*, 78, 1360–1380.
## 8. Scope
Useful when a personal difficulty may involve labor markets, family roles, discrimination, networks, institutions, or unequal resources.
## 9. Limitations
Population patterns do not determine an individual's motives; categories can obscure within-group variation. Avoid treating correlation as individual destiny.
## 10. Applied example
For a career barrier, consider skills and preferences alongside hiring norms, geography, care obligations, credentials, and access to referrals.
## 11. Related theories
Political philosophy; social psychology; economics; organizational sociology; network science.
## 12. References and evidence status
Durkheim (1897), *Suicide* [historical comparative study]; Granovetter (1973) [network theory]; Bourdieu (1986) [theoretical chapter]. Apply with attention to historical and institutional context.
