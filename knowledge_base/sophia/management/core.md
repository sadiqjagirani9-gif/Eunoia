# Management: Goals, Projects, and Coordination

## 1. Theory name
Goal setting, project management, organizational behavior, prioritization, and risk management.
## 2. Proponents
Peter Drucker; Edwin Locke and Gary Latham; Henry Mintzberg; contemporary management researchers.
## 3. Historical background
Management developed alongside industrial organizations; modern approaches combine operations, organizational behavior, strategy, and project methods.
## 4. Core concepts
Specific goals; feedback; scope; milestones; dependencies; capacity; prioritization; coordination; psychological safety; risk register.
## 5. Basic claims
Clear goals and feedback can improve task performance when goals are accepted, feasible, and matched to skills; complex learning tasks may need exploratory goals.
## 6. Research findings
Goal-setting effects are well documented in bounded tasks; transfer to complex personal goals depends on resources, feedback, coordination, and wellbeing.
## 7. Classic research
Locke & Latham (2002), “Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation,” *American Psychologist*, 57, 705–717; Mintzberg (1973), *The Nature of Managerial Work*.
## 8. Scope
Useful for organizing a project or routine, allocating scarce time, and identifying bottlenecks.
## 9. Limitations
Metrics can distort behavior, crowd out intrinsic motives, or hide care and emotional labor. Efficiency is not the only value.
## 10. Applied example
For a month-long goal, define an outcome, weekly milestones, a minimum viable action, required support, and a review date; reduce scope if capacity changes.
## 11. Related theories
Behavioral science; operations research; organizational psychology; decision theory.
## 12. References and evidence status
Locke & Latham (2002) [research synthesis]; Drucker (1954), *The Practice of Management* [management framework]; Mintzberg (1973) [organizational observation]. Translate methods to personal life cautiously.
